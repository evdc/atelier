I

The Frontispiece

A project opens on a title page, not a list. One sentence says what matters now. Everything under it is arranged by its relation to that sentence.

What it breaks with

  • Dashboards count things. This states an intent and shows what serves it.
  • Sessions are not listed. They appear only as marks on the thread of work they belong to.
  • Decisions sit beside the work as first-class objects with an expiry, instead of being buried in chat.

Built from

  • The intent is one line in .atelier/project.md, appended to each session's system prompt when it starts.
  • Threads are works-on links from sessions to tickets.
  • Settled items are notes; "binds" and "cited by" are backlinks.
  • Toward the intent is one judgement per merged change, made by an agent and shown in gold because it is the agent's opinion.

Jank check

  • About twenty rows from three watched views. Nothing here grows with history.

II

The Score

A piece of work drawn the way music is: one staff per session, time running left to right. Lineage, delegation and waiting become shapes you can read at a glance.

What it breaks with

  • A tree view shows who started whom and nothing else. Here the three relations are three different lines.
  • Your own delay is on the page. Every red rest is time an agent spent waiting for you.
  • The playhead moves. Because state is a fold of the log, dragging it back shows the whole app as it was.

Built from

  • Staves and marks come from a small marks projection: one row per turn end, question, answer, proposal and merge.
  • Lines between staves are rows of the links projection: forked-from, workspace-from, spawned-by.
  • The commission is the spawn call's own arguments, kept on the edge.
  • Fork again from here is the per-turn checkpoint plus a native fork.

Jank check

  • One SVG, a few hundred marks for a day of work. Windowed by time, so a month scrolls the same.
  • Scrubbing refolds only the session on screen, from the nearest checkpoint.

III

The Gloss

Code laid out like a glossed manuscript. The left margin says whose hand wrote each passage. The right margin holds what was asked, decided and left open. Below, you can question the author.

What it breaks with

  • Blame names a commit. This names the session, the request behind it, and which lines a person wrote.
  • Code review assumes the author is gone. Here the author can be asked, with everything it knew at the time.
  • Answers don't spend the original session's context. A throwaway copy replies and is archived.

Built from

  • Hands: blame gives the commit; the produced link gives the session; a commit with no session is yours.
  • Glosses are backlinks to the line range, ordered: open questions, decisions, then mentions.
  • Ask the author is ask-a-fork (§11.4), started from a code range instead of a session id.

Jank check

  • Blame is computed once per file and commit, then cached. Margins are fetched for visible lines only.
  • A fork takes seconds to answer. The question stays editable and the answer streams in beneath it.

IV

The Desk

One thing in front of you at a time, chosen by what is held up behind it. The same card on the phone, so a queue can be cleared in the time it takes to wait for coffee.

What it breaks with

  • An inbox is ordered by arrival. This is ordered by cost of delay: how much idle work each answer releases.
  • You never open a transcript to answer. The agent must bring the question, the trade-off and its own lean to the card.
  • It ends. "Nothing needs you" is a state the app wants you to reach, not an empty list to refill.

Built from

  • The queue is pending questions, permissions and proposals: three existing event kinds in one view.
  • What waits behind walks the links projection outward from the asking session: its children, its readers, tickets marked blocks.
  • Record as a decision writes a note and links it to the code the question cited.

Jank check

  • One card rendered. The next is prefetched, so ↵ is a swap, not a load.
  • The phone gets rows of one view and no transcript at all.

V

The Lens

Review by depth, not by file. A change is a short list of things the agent claims are true. Each carries its evidence and opens down to the lines that make it so.

What it breaks with

  • A diff gives every line the same weight. Here attention goes first to what the author doubts.
  • "Approved" hides how much was read. The gauge beside each claim records how deep you actually went.
  • Acceptance is by claim. Four sure claims can land while the doubted one goes back.
  • Some claims are checked by the app, not asserted by the agent, and are marked differently.

Built from

  • Claims are the proposal file's parts, each one its own commit, each written as a sentence.
  • Evidence links to the tool call that produced it, so "12 tests" opens the run.
  • Depth is a per-reader event when a part or hunk is opened: your own reading is in the log too.

Jank check

  • Five rows until you open one. Hunks are fetched and highlighted on open, never before.
  • Changing depth swaps what a row shows; rows are not re-created.
